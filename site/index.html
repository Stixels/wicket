How it works

Four steps. Then it comes back around.

Most support tools stop at the reply. Wicket keeps going until the person who asked knows how it ended.

01

Ask

People ask in your app, in their own words. Wicket notes the page, app version and recent errors.

02

Answer

The assistant answers from your docs and links the exact section it used.

03

Hand off

Anything it can't settle goes to your team, and their reply lands back in the same chat.

04

Hear back

Bugs and ideas become tickets. When one ships, everyone who asked is told.

And the next question starts with someone who trusts you'll get back to them.

Everyone hears back

Nobody's report disappears.

The assistant files bugs, ideas and docs gaps itself. The same problem reported twice lands on one ticket, and every person who reported it gets the news.

  • Repeat reports stack up. A new report on a known problem joins the ticket that already exists, so you see how many people it hits.
  • Reporters get told. When it's fixed, or when an idea is planned or turned down, each reporter gets a message in their chat, and by email if they're away.
  • Say why in one line. Comment Reply: on the ticket and Wicket passes your reason along.

Questions

Good to know.

Do I have to pay for AI?

No. Without an AI key, Wicket is a plain support chat: every conversation goes straight to your team. When you turn the assistant on, you can give each person or account a monthly budget and a total cap, and anonymous visitors can skip the assistant entirely.

Where does my team answer?

In the built-in dashboard at /admin, or in Discord, where each conversation becomes its own private post. Replies show up in the person's chat right away, and by email if they've left.

Which docs can it read?

Any GitHub repo of Markdown (GitBook Git Sync, Docusaurus, Mintlify and similar), or any hosted docs site through its sitemap. It re-indexes on a schedule and links the exact section in each answer.

How does it know which issues to update?

When the assistant files a report, it checks your tracker for the same problem first and adds the person to that ticket if it exists. Wicket then checks the tracker every 15 minutes and messages reporters when a ticket is fixed, planned or turned down. Linear and GitHub Issues work today.

Can it see my users' data?

Only if you let it. App tools are endpoints on your own backend, called with the person's signed token, so your code decides what they can see on every call.
